Are impoverished Americans a caste?

Spoilers yes they are (also systemic racism is in the mix but on some level you already knew that).

So almost a quarter of newborn American children belong to the caste no college degree with only a 20% chance of escaping via education.

You can escape by marriage but it is equally difficult. If you are a woman without a college degree you will have trouble getting married at all much less to a man with a college degree.

If you are a man without a college degree, you maybe can marry a woman with a college degree if you earn enough.

The problem is in early 2026, a man in the U.S. with only a high school diploma typically earns a median of about $47,000–$47,600 per year.

However a child adopted by well educated parents has almost triple the chances of achieving a college degree. In large-scale longitudinal studies (such as the Sibling Interaction and Behavior Study), approximately 55.8% to 58.7% of children adopted into high-education households earn a college degree. That’s incredibly high given that a child up for adoption might have health conditions which impair studying or join his new family later in life.

Is culture to blame?

Traditionally evidence of a caste system is first denied and then, when that fails, there is an attempt to patch up the meritocracy by blaming culture. Unfortunately:

Data from the Federal Reserve’s Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking (SHED) identifies cost as the single most significant factor preventing first-generation students from completing their degrees.

Plus:

Students whose parents have no college experience are disproportionately funneled into 2-year public institutions (53%) or for-profit schools (18%), which often have lower graduation rates and higher debt-to-income ratios than the 4-year non-profit institutions attended by the children of degree holders.

So not surprisingly the caste system is mostly upheld by lack of funds and not lack of culture.

Racially this caste is fairly diverse.

The no college caste is just the latest in a long line of “racism done right” attempts in America.

As of 2023, approximately 26.2% of Black Americans aged 25 or older have earned a bachelor’s degree or higher, an increase from 19.3% in 2013. While attainment is growing, it remains below the national average of 34.8%. The 6-year completion rate for Black students at four-year institutions is roughly 40–46%, often hindered by financial constraints and work responsibilities.

One problem with racism done right is that it’s very difficult to suppress a caste without negatively impacting people not in the caste. For instance slavery negatively impacted white workers in the North by forcing them to sometimes compete with zero wage workers.
