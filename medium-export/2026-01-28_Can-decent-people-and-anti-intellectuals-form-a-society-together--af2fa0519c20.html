Can decent people and anti-intellectuals form a society together?

Some people think that rule of law and a strong economy will be enough to patch up America, but I’m not sure.

I live in Silicon Valley where we have rule of law and a strong economy and yet are also producing people, mostly men, too toxic to exist in a reasonable society.

Stephen Miller is an example of this sort of toxic man and he also comes from a background with rules and money:

At 39, Miller is a millennial (51 percent of voters age 30 to 44 voted for Harris); he was raised Jewish in a Reform congregation (84 percent of Reform Jews voted for Harris) and grew up in Santa Monica, California (Santa Monica’s precincts ranged from 71 to 86 percent for Harris); he has parents with advanced degrees and himself graduated from top-ranked Duke University (56 percent of college graduates and a likely 75 percent of students at Duke voted for Harris)

These men are toxic because, in America at least, it’s not enough to passively go with the flow. One must actively try to be a better, more intellectual and empathetic person or the entire country can fall back to the monstrous slaver nation it once was.

How much and what kind of immigration should America accept? It’s very easy to grab extreme answers like completely open or closed borders but quite hard to educate oneself towards more justified positions.

For certain Silicon Valley was founded on the wrong kind of immigration — importing talented people for profit while education simultaneously became less and less affordable for Americans. At a minimum a reasonable society would take those profits and reinvest them towards a better educated populace.

Equally certain though is that the sort of immigration that allowed Stephen Miller’s maternal grandfather to escape anti-Jewish pogroms is something any empathetic American should support.

For most of my life I have been for lowering the rate of immigration for two reasons:

  1. The high rate of immigration never polled very well and supposedly we live in a democracy.
  2. Bringing more people in while America has such inadequate safety net and educational systems is dangerous.

However with America now at 1.6 births per women, I currently generally positively view anything that would result in more children in this country.

So democracy is tough enough without anti-intellectuals — people who not only don’t want to think about complex issues but are actively opposed to reason, logic, and learning.

I claim that anyone or anything that undermines empathetic, intellectual thinking (IE woke) is a danger to American society and human survival.

Exhibit D — you not only have to teach science but the latest science and adult learning too

If you can, fairly quickly from an evolution point of view, breed a German Shepard to have strong guarding instincts, then why can’t humans vary tremendously from each other in profound ways?

Science has, until recently, been rather supportive of racist thinking. Traditional biology textbooks described genes as a “blueprint for life”. That’s simply not true and modern biologists realize that there are contributions at all human levels — DNA, cells, organs, culture, etc.

The German Shepard example is deeply flawed as you can teach a German Shepard to behave in almost any fashion. Even on base tendencies, what humans call race is nowhere near as defined as purebred German Shepard.

Human history is rife with contentions about the purity (and superiority) of the bloodlines of one group over another and claims over ancestral homelands.
More than a decade of work on ancient human DNA has upended it all.
Instead, Harvard geneticist David Reich said on Monday, increasingly sophisticated analysis of genetic material made possible by technological advances shows that virtually everyone came from somewhere else, and everyone’s genetic background shows a mix from different waves of migration that washed over the globe.

If you’re not subscribed to science and keeping very up to date, you still live in a world where human genetic diversity is way greater then it actually is.

And even if you stay up to date enough to know that race is nonsense, you might still believe in an old fashioned version of gender. You wouldn’t realize that children are gender detectives who implement what they learn:

This basic gender identity knowledge (e.g., I am a girl!) sparks children’s motivation to seek out information about their gender group (e.g., girls play with dolls) so they can match their behavior to their understanding of gender (e.g., I want a doll!; Martin & Ruble, 2010). Children’s active search for gender knowledge leads them to amass a large amount of gender stereotype information throughout early and middle childhood (Martin & Ruble, 2004; Miller et al., 2013). Research examining the development of stereotype knowledge has found that children tend to learn gender stereotypes for activities/toys and appearance by preschool, occupations around age 5, and personality traits throughout the elementary years and into adolescence (Miller et al., 2006; Ruble et al., 2006). More recent research has highlighted the important role of appearance stereotypes and how appearance and personal attractiveness are especially central to girls’ early gender development (Halim et al., 2018, 2024; Miller et al., 2009). The research on developmental trends within specific domains (e.g., activities, appearance, occupations, traits) underscores the multidimensional nature of gender stereotype beliefs (Miller et al., 2024).
