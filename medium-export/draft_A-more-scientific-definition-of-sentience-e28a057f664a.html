A more scientific definition of sentience

  • If you can reconstruct humanity from DNA alone to create a small village and get much the same culture resulting as modern when compare a person from that village to a modern than that is not sentient.
  • Do we even need language in that village? Does language dramatically alter how a human behaves really? https://mcgovern.mit.edu/2019/05/02/ask-the-brain-can-we-think-without-language/ — maybe not
  • What about response to media? If the village had unlimited food and shelter but moderns could enjoy the media because of culture and freshly grown could not would that end up being a major difference? But major difference to what? Procreation and/or war?
  • Is there some base level of culture required for modern human responses and do all moderns even have it?
  • If reconstruction fails because human culture has changed the outcome in unpredictable from DNA ways such that each humanity grown from DNA would end up in a sufficiently large range of outcomes then that is sentience (here ignoring oops we destroyed ourselves with nuclear weapons types of outcomes which don’t need sentience but mostly bad luck).
