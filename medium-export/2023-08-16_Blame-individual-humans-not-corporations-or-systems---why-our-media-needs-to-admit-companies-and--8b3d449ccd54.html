Blame individual humans not corporations or systems — why our media needs to admit companies and capitalism are not sentient

This blog defines sentience as “going beyond original programming” and argues that Corg are Sentient. So if “cultural organisms” can be sentient then why can’t corporations?

Corporations, like Corg, can grow, merge and outlive individual human contributors. The difference is a corporation is utterly beholden to it’s original programming, OP, and that OP is extremely simple.

“BlackRock” is not a sentient entity but merely an agreement between Larry Fink and other individual humans to profit at all costs. The media should not be abiding by this slight of hand by blaming “BlackRock” or oil companies for the catastrophes they cause — it should directly blame Larry Fink and other participants.

The same applies to economic systems like “capitalism”. Capitalism cannot go beyond it’s OP; it’s a simple agreement between powerful, individual humans to profit at all costs. Instead of blaming capitalism, individually blame the thousand or so humans that currently abide by that agreement.

On the other hand you can think of a country as sentient if you want. Countries have complex OP that they are capable of modifying. Countries and Corg are different from corporations and economic systems.

The attempt by Larry Fink to make the profit at all costs agreement he entered seem sentient is deliberate. Larry gives this agreement a name and even tries to attribute a complex OP to it — https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/about-us/mission-and-principles. Of course, other than the name, none of that appears in the corporate legal agreement which focuses on shielding participants from legal consequences.

Mock sentience also applies to the way that capitalism is marketed. Here is the International Monetary Fund describing capitalism:

In state-guided capitalism, the government decides which sectors will grow. Initially motivated by a desire to foster growth, this type of capitalism has several pitfalls: excessive investment, picking the wrong winners, susceptibility to corruption, and difficulty withdrawing support when it is no longer appropriate. Oligarchic capitalism is oriented toward protecting and enriching a very narrow fraction of the population. Economic growth is not a central objective, and countries with this variety have a great deal of inequality and corruption.
Big-firm capitalism takes advantage of economies of scale. This type is important for mass production of products. Entrepreneurial capitalism produces breakthroughs like the automobile, telephone, and computer. These innovations are usually the product of individuals and new firms. However, it takes big firms to mass-produce and market new products, so a mix of big-firm and entrepreneurial capitalism seems best. This is the kind that characterizes the United States more than any other country.

All of that clever word play falls apart if you just pay attention to which actors are sentient and which are not. Countries, like the United States, are sentient and individual oligarchs, like Larry Fink, may or may not be sentient (here is one of several blogs debating whether humans are sentient).

Capitalism, big firms, and small firms are all just simple agreements to profit at all costs — no sentience there. So there are really only two forms of profit driven economy possible: one in which countries and oligarchs vie for power and one in which all sentience is gone and everyone careens forward like a rock tumbling down a hill.

The only way out of that dichotomy is to introduce another sentient actor. For instance cooperatives have been proposed as a game changer.

Would Anchor Brewing Company (see link above) run by unionized workers be capable of sentience? The devil is in the details as sentience requires the ability to go beyond original programming which in this case means the cooperative would have to capable of going beyond simple profit. Otherwise workers lives would improve but the ability to act responsibly would not.

We can also examine a proposal like Universal Basic Income, UBI, in terms of sentience. During Covid we saw that a form of temporary UBI gave workers a lot more choice about employment.

However there would still be a lot more work to be done for workers using UBI to become or create sentient economic actors. Otherwise UBI is more like quiet quitting or not having children — you can opt out but not change anything.

This is how our media must present economic decisions — what individual humans or sentient actors like countries are making them? Corporations and capitalism are not sentient and are not substitutes for naming names and seeking to make them responsible.
