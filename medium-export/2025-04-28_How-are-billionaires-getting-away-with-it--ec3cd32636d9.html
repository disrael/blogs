How are billionaires getting away with it?

If having a billion dollars is the moral equivalent of slavery, as this blog contends, then why would any American like a billionaire?

Take Warren Buffett at 52% favorable and only 26% not favorable. People must be aware of how billionaires like Buffet wage class warfare in general. But Buffett’s corruption is an open secret.

For instance he actively supported the 2008 financial bailouts of “too big to fail” banks because he was invested heavily in one:

Nor was that even Buffett’s first time making sure the government supported his investments.

In 1987, Berkshire Hathaway had acquired a 12% interest in the investment banking firm Salomon Brothers.c
In 1990, the news came out that a rogue trader had submitted bids in excess of Treasury rules and CEO John Gutfreund had failed to take disciplinary action. The U.S. government threatened to hold Salomon accountable. Buffett stepped into the breach; he directly intervened with the U.S. Treasury to reverse a ban on Salomon bidding in government bond auctions.

Buffett famously signed The Giving Pledge, and has given about $43 billion to the Gates Foundation.

First, as this blog previously explained, ill gotten gains cannot be redeemed by charity anymore than slave owners could justify owning slaves with philanthropy. Second, where billionaires use their wealth and power to lean heavily on the laws and governance of a society to support the wealthy, their charities make no effort to reverse this policy damage.

Ross Perot maybe came closest with his run for president:

In 1992, Perot announced his intention to run for president and advocated a balanced budget, an end to the outsourcing of jobs, and the enactment of electronic direct democracy.

However a closer look at his plans casts doubt that he would do much:

The most prominent element of the plan was Perot’s proposal to raise the income tax bracket of 4% wealthiest households from 31 to 33 percent, and to raise it further to 35 percent in the future.

There is no hint of Perot embracing Limitarianism, and, as with slavery, one either breaks with allowing extreme inequality or doesn’t.

In 2026, everyone can experience the havoc that billionaires like Trump and Musk can wreak on America. An amount of money that threatens our nation is clearly too much for a person to have.

However, if you limit everyone to under a billion dollars or even under 20 million dollars then how do we prevent multitudes of millionaires from crushing it at class warfare?

According to the 2022 Survey of Consumer Finances by the Federal Reserve, only about 12% of U.S. households have a net worth over $1 million. Slaying the oligarchy head of the Cerberus could conceivably make the gerontocracy and meritocracy heads even worse.

There’s Obama chiding that you are not educated enough for the global economy and now here’s Trump offering to end the global economy.

Alexandria co-sponsored the College for All Act, a law that will establish tuition-free public college and trade school, and through the Student Debt Cancellation Act, we will forgive outstanding federal and private student loans of all previous and current students in our education system.

“It wasn’t until I took a high-stakes test where I scored in the 99th percentile across the board where they figured out I did not need remedial education,” AOC told the crowd. “It took a test instead of understanding the child in front of them.”

If you prefer non-whites be kept second class regardless of aptitude than meritocracy is more the enemy. If you prefer young people be kept second class regardless of aptitude than meritocracy is again your enemy.

On the other hand AOC is no Barack Obama. These superiority claims go back to a cultish like philosophy that the billionaires have been pushing for a long time. In this cult, money and power are their own objective outside of any utility to society.

I don’t think in making an investment decision on Berkshire Hathaway, or most companies — virtually all of the companies I can think of — that climate change should be a factor in the decision-making process.

This is exactly the slave trader ethos — do whatever you are allowed to do and don’t worry about it. Last week! this plaque was installed in Bristol:

The statue was put up after the end of the American civil war in 1865, 174 years after Colston’s death, as a statement piece.

Why would the people of Bristol, who were no longer benefiting from Colston — assuming they ever did, want a statue of him displayed prominently? It’s not American style racism as there were very few blacks living in Bristol in 1895 (now ethnic people are almost 20% and so the statue came down). No it’s pride.

Americans are proud of their billionaires and want to give them the benefit of the doubt — right up until it’s no longer reasonably possible and maybe a bit further. There is no national American identity feeling pride around unions, child labor laws, women’s suffrage, or 5 day work weeks because we are not currently very progressively exceptional.

America was never going to be a capital of history and culture like London, Paris, or Rome. Aside from Hollywood, what we exceptionally have is banks, tech, oil, and billionaires (who come in two flavors — tech and financial). Even when we used to have manufacturing that was tech also.

Much has been made of woke this or that, but the American, and in some ways global, identity crisis would be there even if humanity were all one race, gender, and nationality. We are no longer about reproduce at all costs so if we are not about religion or “innovation” then who are we?
