The origin of California higher education tuition

Before the Regents’ vote in 1970, the University of California had been tuition-free since its founding in 1868.

Ronald Reagan introduced tuition to prevent student unrest, arguing that it

might affect those who are there really not to study but to agitate, it might make them think twice about paying a fee for the privilege of carrying a picket sign.

Tuition was the fascist solution to student protest, but it became the tip of the spear in the creation of an educational caste system.

Reagan also frequently compared campus radicals to Nazi brownshirts, arguing there was “no longer any room for appeasement.”
