Respect for Sale is a Fail

We have never, in all of history, been this close to having everything be for sale. In the past it was difficult to buy your way into royalty, become the next pope, or purchase common land. Not impossible but very difficult.

In the past rich people were unlikely to make there way out to buy up an East Solano, because travel was so hard and unpleasant. In the past just the logistics of acquiring and moving large amounts of gold while avoiding thieves was fairly prohibitive.

In a feudal society a noble had a lot of control over his portion of the kingdom but that power didn’t transfer to other areas. That’s an important distinction because at the end of the day you had to live where you ruled. 

The freedom to go anywhere and buy anything will backfire and potentially in an almost existential way. There are just too many possibilities — maybe a Jeffrey Epstein type blackmails a president into something terrible, perhaps a billionaire successfully reintroduces some form of generational debt slavery, or oil company execs buy off the government or public opinion to keep destroying the climate (if that hasn’t already happened).

How long before privatization results in a mass poisoning or environmental catastrophe? Before a Golden Gate Bridge or section of ocean is owned by an individual? Before, in an effort to live forever, a billionaire pays to experiment on thousands of humans? Before a billionaire becomes the dictator of a large country?

The problem is that the bar for concern for future threats is currently very high and we frequently choose to believe that rich people are innovators despite any number of Wikipedia entries, books, and articles detailing otherwise. The ultra wealthy as innovators is a canard, but even were it true there would be no justification for handing out the power they wield.

However even in a society with no billionaires, everything for sale simply cannot work. First and foremost respect cannot be for sale. This is the big one — the one that’s destroying our society as you read this.

The undeserved massive rewards pulled in by businessmen are toxic in many ways:

  • Fields like science, governance, health care, and others become less respected and it’s participants grifters seeking their fortunes.
  • It destroys democracy and respect for the institutions it requires.
  • It de-incentivizes people who actually innovate to stop doing so unless outrageous fortunes are possible for their contributions.
  • It financializes our economy — as the economy is warped to produce investments for the undeserved fortunes and mainstream media and governments reflect financialized preferences.
  • It destroys community because community requires mutual respect.
