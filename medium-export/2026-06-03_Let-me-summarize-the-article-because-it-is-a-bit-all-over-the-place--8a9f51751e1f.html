Let me summarize the article because it is a bit all over the place:

1. A man walks into a tree because he doesn't look up from scrolling silly stuff

2. In the hospital his friend is not concerned for his bleeding eye but that she had something really important to say that he didn't listen to

3. "technology was producing a culture of self-referential noise, where everyone is broadcasting and almost nobody is receiving." - but what are they broadcasting? They are broadcasting very low quality content as was explained in the first part.

4. "We stopped practicing the small, low-stakes interactions that keep those muscles working — the chat with the cashier, the nod to the neighbor, the accidental eye contact that becomes a smile." - no that's not what happened in her story at all. They were walking together and both were trying to connect. Her friend was unsuccessfully trying to connect with very low quality content he was reading on the internet. The author was trying to connect with some real problem she was having - not low quality content.

5. "Critical thinking doesn’t thrive in that environment. Neither does deep conversation. When your attention has been fractured by years of short-form everything, sitting with a complex idea, or a complex person, becomes genuinely hard work." Now we are back on track. More short form, rudimentary communication is not the solution to a lack of critical thinking.

6. "Empathy takes effort and we’re tired. Community requires showing up and we’re busy. So we retreat, outsource, interact through screens, and slowly the human software starts to glitch." Again fully on track here empathy and community are the opposite of attempting small talk with complete strangers.

But look I don't want to be guilty of being too adversarial; another issue mentioned in the article. If you believe openness to brief human connection is the answer then you might be right and Mona's article has it both ways on many points - phones aren't the root problem but also they are.
